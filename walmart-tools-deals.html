What to check before buying Tools & DIY deals

Tool prices are easy to misjudge because the same name can mean very different things: a bare tool with no battery, a kit with a battery and charger, or a combo set of several tools. Before you compare prices, make sure you are comparing the same package.

For cordless tools, the battery platform matters more than any single discount. Batteries and chargers usually only work within one brand's system, so a cheap tool that does not match the batteries you already own can end up costing more than it saves. Check the voltage and the amp-hour rating on the battery, and note whether the battery and charger are included. For corded tools, look at the power rating and the cord length. Read what the listing says about the warranty, and keep the receipt and box until you have tried the tool.

If you only need a tool for one project, a modest price may beat a premium model you will rarely use. If you will use it often, spend your attention on the features that affect daily use rather than on the size of the markdown.

  • Bare tool, kit or combo set?
  • Does it match your battery platform?
  • Voltage and amp-hours on the battery
  • Warranty wording in the listing

Tools & DIY deals FAQ

What does bare tool mean?

It means the tool is sold without a battery or charger. It can look much cheaper than a kit, so compare it only against other bare tools, or add the cost of a battery and charger to compare it with a kit.

Are cordless tools from different brands compatible?

Usually not. Batteries and chargers are generally designed for a single brand's system, so check which battery a tool takes before you buy.
